Research

My research focuses on mathematical and computational descriptions of transport in heterogeneous porous media, connecting pore-scale particle dynamics with stochastic and continuum-scale models.

Research Overview

I study anomalous transport in complex porous environments, with particular interest in elongated, deformable, and thread-like particles such as microplastic fibers. Their transport can differ substantially from the behavior predicted by classical diffusion and advection-diffusion models.

My work seeks to understand how pore geometry, particle-scale dynamics, trapping and retention, heterogeneous transition times, and temporal memory collectively influence macroscopic transport and breakthrough behavior.

To address these problems, I combine pore-scale modeling with stochastic upscaling, memory-dependent transport formulations, first-passage-time analysis, Laplace-transform techniques, and semi-analytical methods.

Current Research Themes

01

Microfiber and Microplastic Transport in Porous Media

I investigate the transport of elongated and deformable particles through geometrically complex porous structures. Unlike ideal spherical tracers, thread-like particles interact with pore geometry through their orientation, deformation, and particle–fluid–solid interactions.

A central objective is to determine how these microscopic mechanisms affect retention, residence times, spreading, and breakthrough at larger scales.

Microplastics Microfibers Porous Media Pore-Scale Dynamics
02

Anomalous Transport and Stochastic Upscaling

Transport through heterogeneous pore networks may exhibit broad transition-time distributions and non-Fickian behavior. I use stochastic transport frameworks, particularly Continuous Time Random Walk models, to connect pore-scale transition statistics with macroscopic transport.

These models provide a framework for representing heterogeneous travel times, delayed transport, retention, and long-tailed breakthrough behavior.

CTRW Anomalous Transport Stochastic Processes Upscaling
03

Memory-Dependent Transport

My research examines transport models in which the present evolution depends on the history of the transport process. Memory kernels provide a mathematical mechanism for incorporating non-Markovian effects associated with heterogeneous waiting and transition times.

I study how different memory structures influence concentration evolution, breakthrough curves, and long-time transport behavior.

Memory Kernels Non-Markovian Dynamics Breakthrough Curves Heavy Tails
04

First-Passage-Time and Breakthrough Analysis

First-passage quantities provide a natural way to characterize when particles reach absorbing boundaries, exit a porous region, or encounter geometrical obstacles. I study first-passage and complementary breakthrough behavior in domains containing circular cavities and multiple obstacles.

These quantities provide insight into characteristic transport times, long-time tails, and the influence of geometry on particle migration.

First-Passage Time Survival Probability Breakthrough Diffusion
05

Semi-Analytical Methods for Complex Geometry

I develop semi-analytical approaches for diffusion and first-passage problems involving circular boundaries and multiple cavities. The formulation combines Laplace transforms, Fourier representations, modified Bessel functions, and multipole expansions.

These methods provide an alternative to purely numerical discretization and allow geometric interactions to be studied through structured mathematical representations.

Laplace Transform Multipole Expansion Bessel Functions Semi-Analytical Methods

Current Direction

Geometry, Memory, and Transport Across Scales

My current work extends first-passage and memory-dependent transport formulations to systems containing multiple circular obstacles and increasingly heterogeneous geometries. The broader objective is to develop mathematically interpretable models that connect local geometric interactions and temporal memory with observable breakthrough behavior at larger scales.
