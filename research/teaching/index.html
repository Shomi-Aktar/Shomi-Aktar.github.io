Teaching & Mentoring

Teaching

My teaching is centered on clear mathematical reasoning, active problem solving, and helping students develop both computational proficiency and a deeper understanding of why mathematical ideas work.

Teaching Philosophy

I view mathematics as a subject that becomes meaningful when students can connect procedures with the ideas that justify them. My goal is therefore not simply to demonstrate how to solve a problem, but to help students understand the mathematical structure behind the solution.

In the classroom, I combine structured explanations with worked examples, visual representations, guided questions, and opportunities for students to participate in the problem-solving process. I use these approaches to make abstract concepts more accessible while maintaining mathematical precision.

I also recognize that students enter mathematics courses with different levels of preparation and confidence. I strive to create a supportive environment in which students can ask questions, learn from mistakes, and gradually become more independent mathematical thinkers.

My teaching continues to evolve through student interaction, reflection, and feedback. I use these experiences to refine the pacing, organization, examples, and explanations I bring to each course.

“Students should leave a mathematics course knowing not only how to perform a calculation, but how to reason about the mathematics behind it.”

Teaching Approach

01

Conceptual Understanding

Connect mathematical procedures with definitions, theorems, geometry, and underlying ideas.

02

Guided Problem Solving

Develop mathematical reasoning through carefully structured examples and step-by-step analysis.

03

Visual Reasoning

Use graphs, diagrams, and computational tools when they clarify abstract mathematical relationships.

04

Continuous Improvement

Use classroom experience and student feedback to improve pacing, explanations, and course organization.
