About Me

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Mathematics at The University of Alabama, where I also earned an M.S. in Applied Mathematics. I received my B.S. and M.S. degrees in Mathematics from the University of Chittagong, Bangladesh, graduating with distinction and receiving the Gold Medal for Academic Excellence for my outstanding academic performance.

As a Ph.D. candidate under the supervision of Dr. Mojdeh Rasoulzadeh, I study the transport and dynamics of thread-like deformable particles in complex and microstructured porous media. My research combines mathematical and numerical methods with stochastic modeling to understand particle transport across scales, with particular interest in anomalous transport, memory effects, and first-passage dynamics.

My academic achievements have also been recognized through a Ministry of Science and Technology Fellowship from the Government of Bangladesh, along with academic scholarships and research support. At the University of Chittagong, I received research funding to conduct a project supported by the University's Research and Publication Cell.

Before beginning my doctoral studies in the United States, I gained more than a decade of university-level teaching and academic experience in Bangladesh, progressing from Lecturer to Assistant Professor. Alongside teaching and research, I contributed to academic service and leadership, including organizing the National Undergraduate Mathematics Olympiad and serving in leadership roles for major departmental activities.

At The University of Alabama, I continue to develop as both a researcher and educator, and I have received multiple competitive travel awards and funding to present my research at national and international professional meetings.

Research Interests

Anomalous Transport

Mathematical modeling of non-Fickian transport, breakthrough behavior, first-passage processes, and memory-dependent dynamics.

Microplastics in Porous Media

Transport of microfiber and thread-like particles through geometrically complex porous environments across pore and continuum scales.

Stochastic Modeling

Continuous-time random walks, memory kernels, residence-time distributions, and stochastic descriptions of heterogeneous transport.

First-Passage Problems

First-passage-time distributions and boundary-value formulations for diffusion and transport in geometrically heterogeneous domains.

Semi-Analytical Methods

Laplace-transform techniques, multipole expansions, special-function representations, and analytical-numerical approaches for transport problems.

Scientific Computing

Numerical computation and simulation for mathematical models arising in transport phenomena, porous media, and applied stochastic processes.
