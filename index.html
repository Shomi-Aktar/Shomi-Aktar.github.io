About Me

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Mathematics at The University of Alabama. My research lies at the intersection of applied mathematics, stochastic processes, transport phenomena, and scientific computing.

My current work investigates transport of elongated, thread-like microplastic particles through porous media. I am particularly interested in how pore-scale geometry, particle dynamics, and memory effects influence breakthrough behavior and first-passage-time distributions.

My research combines mathematical modeling, semi-analytical methods, stochastic transport models, and numerical computation to connect detailed pore-scale dynamics with effective macroscopic descriptions.

Alongside research, I have extensive experience teaching undergraduate mathematics and remain strongly interested in effective mathematical instruction and student learning.

Research Interests

Anomalous Transport

Mathematical modeling of non-Fickian transport, breakthrough behavior, first-passage processes, and memory-dependent dynamics.

Microplastics in Porous Media

Transport of microfiber and thread-like particles through geometrically complex porous environments across pore and continuum scales.

Stochastic Modeling

Continuous-time random walks, memory kernels, residence-time distributions, and stochastic descriptions of heterogeneous transport.

First-Passage Problems

First-passage-time distributions and boundary-value formulations for diffusion and transport in geometrically heterogeneous domains.

Semi-Analytical Methods

Laplace-transform techniques, multipole expansions, special-function representations, and analytical-numerical approaches for transport problems.

Scientific Computing

Numerical computation and simulation for mathematical models arising in transport phenomena, porous media, and applied stochastic processes.
