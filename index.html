About Me

Advised by Prof. Mojdeh Rasoulzadeh, I study anomalous transport of deformable particles in porous media, with applications to microplastic fibers.

I study how geometric heterogeneity and temporal memory influence particle migration, retention, and arrival times. My work combines stochastic modeling with semi-analytical methods to connect local particle behavior with transport at larger scales.

My academic experience spans university teaching and student research supervision in Bangladesh and the United States. I previously served as an Assistant Professor of Mathematics and now teach as an Instructor of Record.

Research Interests

Anomalous Transport

Non-Fickian spreading and delayed arrival of particles in heterogeneous porous environments.

Microplastic Fibers

Migration and retention of elongated, deformable particles around circular obstacles.

Stochastic Modeling

Continuous-time random walks and waiting-time distributions for heterogeneous particle motion.

First-Passage Analysis

Time to reach a boundary or exit a region, and its relationship to breakthrough behavior.

Semi-Analytical Methods

Laplace transforms, Fourier representations, Bessel functions, and multipole expansions.

Scientific Computing

Numerical analysis and simulation of diffusion and transport models.
